# Research Report: Comparison of Sparse Retrieval (BM25) vs. Dense Retrieval Methods

## Executive Summary
This report synthesizes findings on the comparative performance of sparse retrieval methods, particularly BM25, and dense retrieval methods. The analysis focuses on their underlying algorithms, strengths and weaknesses in various scenarios, and recent advancements in both methodologies. The evidence suggests that while both retrieval methods have their unique advantages, their effectiveness can vary significantly based on the context of use.

## Findings

### What are the fundamental differences between sparse retrieval methods, such as BM25, and dense retrieval methods in terms of their underlying algorithms?
Evidence suggests that sparse retrieval methods like BM25 rely on term-based ranking, while dense retrieval methods utilize contextualized embeddings generated by neural networks. Dense retrieval methods can leverage pre-trained transformer models to enhance retrieval effectiveness, although this may introduce additional complexity in terms of computational requirements [Source: On the Interpolation of Contextualized Term-based Ranking with BM25 for Query-by-Example Retrieval].

### In what scenarios or types of data do sparse retrieval methods like BM25 outperform dense retrieval methods?
There is disagreement about the specific scenarios where sparse retrieval methods excel. Some evidence suggests that BM25 may outperform dense methods for definitional queries and those with complex information needs [Source: On Single and Multiple Representations in Dense Passage Retrieval]. However, the exact contexts remain uncertain, as dense retrieval methods also show promise in similar situations.

### What are the advantages of dense retrieval methods over sparse retrieval methods in terms of performance and scalability?
Dense retrieval methods are increasingly recognized for their efficiency and high performance, particularly in scoring question-passage pairs. They are becoming a standard approach for document and passage ranking due to their ability to handle large datasets effectively [Source: Analysing the Robustness of Dual Encoders for Dense Retrieval Against Misspellings]. However, their performance can significantly deteriorate when exposed to noisy text, which poses a challenge for their scalability [Source: Analysing the Robustness of Dual Encoders for Dense Retrieval Against Misspellings].

### How do the evaluation metrics differ when assessing the effectiveness of sparse versus dense retrieval methods?
There is currently insufficient evidence to draw definitive conclusions about the differences in evaluation metrics for sparse and dense retrieval methods. Existing studies suggest that adapting metrics from binary information retrieval for segment-based tasks may be beneficial, but specific comparative metrics remain unclear [Source: Adapting Binary Information Retrieval Evaluation Metrics for Segment-based Retrieval Tasks].

### What recent advancements or trends have emerged in both sparse and dense retrieval methods that impact their comparative performance?
Recent advancements indicate that the integration of contextualized language models can enhance the effectiveness of both sparse and dense retrieval methods. Techniques such as self-knowledge distillation and joint sparse-dense optimization are emerging trends that aim to improve retrieval accuracy while managing computational complexity [Source: Sparse and Dense Retrievers Learn Better Together: Joint Sparse-Dense Optimization for Text-Image Retrieval].

## Contradictions & Disagreements
- There is disagreement about the scenarios where BM25 outperforms dense retrieval methods. While some studies suggest BM25 is superior for definitional queries, others indicate that dense methods can match or exceed this performance in similar contexts.
- The effectiveness of dense retrieval methods can vary significantly based on the quality of input data, with some evidence indicating that their performance deteriorates in the presence of noisy text, which contrasts with the more robust performance of sparse methods in such scenarios.

## Known Gaps & Limitations
- There is a lack of consensus on the specific contexts in which sparse retrieval methods outperform dense methods, necessitating further research to clarify these scenarios.
- The evaluation metrics for comparing sparse and dense retrieval methods are not well-defined, indicating a need for standardized metrics that can effectively assess both methodologies.
- Recent advancements in retrieval techniques, while promising, require more empirical validation to establish their effectiveness across diverse datasets and query types.
