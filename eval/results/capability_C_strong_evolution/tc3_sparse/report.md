## Executive Summary

Sparse retrieval methods such as BM25 and dense retrieval methods differ fundamentally in their representation of text and retrieval mechanisms. Sparse methods rely on exact keyword matching and are highly efficient, making them suitable for scenarios where precision and interpretability are paramount, such as legal or technical document search. Dense retrieval methods, often powered by neural embeddings from models like BERT, excel at capturing semantic similarity, enabling retrieval of relevant content even without exact term overlap. On standard benchmarks like MS MARCO and TREC 2019, dense retrieval models generally outperform BM25, especially for complex or paraphrased queries. However, BM25 can outperform dense methods in domains with specialized terminology or for queries requiring exact matches. Hybrid systems that combine both approaches often achieve the best results, leveraging the complementary strengths of each. Dense methods typically require more computational resources, particularly GPUs, and incur higher latency and cost, while BM25 remains cost-effective and fast. The choice between methods should consider the nature of the queries, domain specificity, computational constraints, and the importance of semantic versus exact matching.
