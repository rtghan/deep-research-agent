## Executive Summary

Chain-of-thought (CoT) prompting is a prompt engineering technique that encourages large language models (LLMs) to break down their reasoning into a series of intermediate steps. Introduced and popularized by Wei et al. in 2022, CoT prompting has been shown to improve performance on certain multi-step reasoning tasks, particularly in larger models and in domains such as math word problems, commonsense reasoning, and symbolic manipulation. However, the improvements are often modest and are not consistently observed across all tasks or model types. The effectiveness of CoT prompting is highly dependent on the quality of the prompts, the scale of the model, and the nature of the task. 

Research has identified several limitations and open questions, including error propagation, computational overhead, and a lack of comprehensive theoretical frameworks explaining CoT’s success. While some studies suggest that even random or invalid reasoning steps can sometimes yield performance gains, these effects are not universal and depend heavily on context. There is no strong evidence of active disagreement among researchers regarding the basic limitations of CoT, but there is ongoing debate and exploration of its robustness, scalability, and applicability to tasks beyond language-based reasoning. New frameworks, such as DiffCoT, have been proposed to address some of these challenges.

- Chain of Thought prompting requires the model to explain how it arrived at the final answer, offering more transparency and potentially improving accuracy as a theoretical benefit, though consistent accuracy improvements have not been universally demonstrated [confidence: 0.82 · supported · Source: Chain of Thought Prompting Guide].
- Chain of Thought prompting enables large language models to decompose complex problems into a series of intermediate steps, allowing the model to focus on each step individually, though there is no direct evidence that it changes the model's internal attention mechanisms [confidence: 0.90 · supported · Source: Chain of Thought Prompting Guide].
- Chain of Thought prompting leverages large language models to articulate a succession of reasoning steps, guiding the model toward generating analogous reasoning chains for novel tasks, though the evidence does not specify the effectiveness or reliability of this guidance across all types of novel tasks [confidence: 0.89 · supported · Source: What is chain of thought (CoT) prompting?].
- Evidence suggests that Chain of Thought prompting can be achieved not only through exemplar-based prompts but also by explicitly instructing the model to reason step by step, as the method generally refers to eliciting intermediate reasoning steps rather than being limited to exemplars [confidence: 0.59 · insufficient · Source: What is chain of thought (CoT) prompting?].

### Empirical Evidence for Effectiveness Across Tasks

- Chain of thought prompting can improve performance on various reasoning tasks, though these improvements are often small and are more pronounced in larger models and for tasks that can be solved through language-based reasoning (e.g., math word problems, commonsense reasoning, and symbolic manipulation) [confidence: 0.95 · supported · Source: Language Models Perform Reasoning via Chain of Thought].
- Successful chain of thought reasoning is observed as an emergent property of model scale on tasks such as CommonsenseQA, StrategyQA, and Date Understanding, though this has only been empirically demonstrated for a limited set of tasks and models [confidence: 0.82 · supported · Source: Language Models Perform Reasoning via Chain of Thought].
- For CommonsenseQA, StrategyQA, and Date Understanding, performance improved with model scale, and employing chain of thought prompting led to additional small improvements [confidence: 0.89 · supported · Source: Language Models Perform Reasoning via Chain of Thought].
- Chain of Thought prompting has been successfully tested across a diverse set of language-based reasoning tasks, such as math word problems, commonsense reasoning, and symbolic manipulation, but its effectiveness is limited to tasks that can be solved through language and has not been demonstrated across non-language or non-reasoning domains [confidence: 0.81 · supported · Source: Chain of Thought Prompting Guide].
- Increasing the number of reasoning steps for challenging tasks can improve performance in reasoning models [confidence: 0.65 · supported · Source: Chain of Thought Prompting Guide].
- Explicitly instructing the model to spend more time reasoning correlates with higher accuracy and better outputs [confidence: 0.65 · supported · Source: Chain of Thought Prompting Guide].
- Chain of Thought Prompting is applicable across various tasks, including math word problems, commonsense reasoning, and symbolic manipulation [confidence: 0.65 · supported · Source: Master Prompting Concepts: Chain of Thought Prompting].
- Chain of Thought Prompting is most effective when applied to tasks that can be solved through language [confidence: 0.65 · supported · Source: Master Prompting Concepts: Chain of Thought Prompting].
- The effectiveness of Chain of Thought Prompting is influenced by the quality of the prompts used, but other factors such as model scale and the number of reasoning steps also play important roles in determining its overall effectiveness [confidence: 0.90 · supported · Source: Master Prompting Concepts: Chain of Thought Prompting].

### Criticisms and Limitations

- Existing research on chain-of-thought (CoT) prompting primarily focuses on improving performance and generally lacks a comprehensive framework to explain and understand the fundamental factors behind CoT’s success, though recent work such as the Hopfieldian view has begun to propose theoretical frameworks for explanation [confidence: 0.77 · supported · Source: Understanding Reasoning in Chain-of-Thought from the Hopfieldian View].
- Most current approaches to interpreting CoT reasoning focus on representation-level analysis without considering how these representations connect to concepts learned during pre-training, though some works do investigate concept localization and related issues [confidence: 0.85 · supported · Source: Understanding Reasoning in Chain-of-Thought from the Hopfieldian View].
